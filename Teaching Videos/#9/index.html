TEACHING VIDEO #9

Let's make scrolling more fun.

Scroll-driven animations let us animate elements based on how far we scroll. And the best part? We can do it with CSS!

Let's explore ↓
THE BASICS

Animations that follow your scroll.

Normally, CSS animations run based on time. Scroll-driven animations are different because scrolling controls their progress.

01

Normal Animation

Uses time to decide how quickly an animation happens.

animation-duration: 2s;
02

Scroll Animation

Uses your scroll position instead of a timer.

animation-timeline: scroll();
EXAMPLE ONE

Meet scroll().

See that little blue line at the very top of the page?

That is our reading progress bar! As you scroll down, it fills up. When you scroll back up, it shrinks.

animation-timeline: scroll(root);
EXAMPLE TWO

Now let's try view().

These cards fade in and slide up as they enter your screen. Each one has its own animation!

✦
01 / SCROLL

Keep scrolling

This card starts animating when it enters the viewport.

✳
02 / VIEW

Watch me appear

The animation follows this card's position on your screen.

✿
03 / RANGE

Perfect timing

Animation-range controls when the animation starts and ends.

✧
04 / CSS

No JavaScript!

All of these effects are made using CSS animations.

EXAMPLE THREE

Control when it happens.

We can use animation-range to choose which part of scrolling controls the animation.

For example, entry means the animation happens while the element is entering the screen.

animation-range: entry 0% entry 60%;
↗

Here I am!

I fade in while entering the screen.

THAT'S A WRAP

Pretty cool, right?

Scroll-driven animations make websites feel more interactive without needing JavaScript scroll listeners.
